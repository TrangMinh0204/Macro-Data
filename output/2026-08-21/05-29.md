## 5 U.S. Deportees Restrained After Resisting Deportation to Liberia
The five men were part of the first group of deportees to be sent to the West African nation by the Trump administration. The Liberian government has not clarified their status.

Aug. 20, 2026 Updated 6:18 p.m. ET
Five of the 20 U.S. deportees who arrived in Liberia on Thursday as part of a deportation deal with the Trump administration refused to deplane and had to be restrained for several minutes by American federal agents, according to two people with knowledge of the situation.
The 20 deportees arrived at Roberts International Airport on Thursday afternoon in the Liberian capital, Monrovia, according to Liberia’s foreign minister, Sara Beysolow Nyanti.
American federal agents escorting the deportees forced the five resisting men off the plane, but eventually all five were returned to the aircraft, according to three people who spoke on the condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the operation.
The Liberian government has not clarified the status of the five individuals. The State Department referred questions about the operation to the Department of Homeland Security, which did not respond to requests for comment. A video verified by The New York Times shows some of the deportees sitting on the airport tarmac next to the plane, surrounded by roughly 20 agents.
Credit Credit...Obtained by The Times
The deportees arrived in Liberia amid a growing backlash in Africa, with critics saying the Liberian government’s deal with the Trump administration to accept 1,200 U.S. deportees is too large and too opaque. And the resistance among some of the deportees underscored how the Trump administration’s efforts to expel migrants to countries where they have no ties has caused fear and uncertainty, rights groups say.
