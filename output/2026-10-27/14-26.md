📌 **Nội dung đầy đủ:**
Title: Ex-FBI chief James Comey faces federal charges for third time
URL Source: https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckly0xqjnlwvo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss
Published Time: 2026-10-26T21:59:28.995Z
Federal prosecutors have taken steps to charge ex-Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Director James Comey with leaking national defence information, according to a court filing.
It marks the third time the justice department has moved to bring criminal charges against Comey, a longtime critic of President Donald Trump.
Few details are provided about the two counts in the three-page filing, but Comey's lawyers said in separate court papers that the case stemmed from an alleged disclosure of classified information to the New York Times in October 2016, while he was head of the FBI.
Comey denied any wrongdoing in a video posted on Substack on Monday, calling the charges an "appalling abuse".
"It would almost be funny if it weren't such an appalling abuse of the department of justice, one that we cannot become numb to," he said in the clip.
"Nothing's changed with me - I'm still innocent, I'm still not afraid of these people and I still believe in the independent federal judiciary."
Comey was charged in Washington DC through a criminal information, which is a formal written charging document filed directly by a prosecutor.
This is different to a criminal indictment, which must be approved by a grand jury, a group of citizens sworn in to review if evidence is strong enough to prosecute.
Comey's lawyer, Patrick Fitzgerald, said in a statement that two different teams of prosecutors had investigated the alleged disclosure to the New York Times a decade ago during the first Trump administration.
"Both times the Government determined that no charges should be brought against anyone," he said.
The attorney said the court filing "purports to charge Mr Comey with crimes that he did not commit".
His legal team has filed a motion to dismiss the charges.
Former federal prosecutor Glenn Kirschner told the BBC that the case may have exceeded the 10-year statute of limitations, giving the motion to dismiss the charges "a very good chance of being granted".
Kirschner said it seemed like the charges against Comey were a "revenge-fuelled attempt by Donald Trump to have his Department of Justice find something that might stick".
![Image 1: Getty Images James Comey speaks onstage during Former FBI Director James Comey In Conversation With MSNBC's Nicolle Wallace at 92NY on May 30, 2023 in New York City.
](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/news/480/cpsprodpb/21c9/live/16bb9ba0-d187-11f1-8d7e-c9ff4ede405b.jpg.webp)Getty Images
Comey was fired by Trump during his first term, after the former FBI director opened an investigation into claims of Russian meddling in the US 2016 presidential election.
Since then, Trump has repeatedly called for his prosecution, and these charges are the administration's third attempt to do so.
Comey was first indicted by a federal grand jury in September 2025 on charges that he had lied to Congress during testimony and obstructed a congressional proceeding.
He pleaded not guilty the following month before the case was dismissed in November.
In April 2026, Comey surrendered to authorities to face charges alleging that an image he briefly shared on social media posed a threat to Trump's life.

The White House is confronting a familiar dilemma. The economic numbers aren't necessarily bad - but the public mood is.
It's a disconnect that haunted Joe Biden in 2024, when solid headline data failed to convince many voters that the economy was working for them.
Now, Donald Trump faces a version of the same challenge.
During the 2024 campaign, Trump promised voters swift relief from rising prices.
"When I win, I will immediately bring prices down," he said.
Delivering on that promise has proved harder than expected.
Economic growth is holding up, unemployment remains low and the stock market is near record highs.
Yet the final inflation report ahead of the midterm elections confirms what voters already know: [prices remain high](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwy8lx3pg4k2o).
The causes are different. Biden was grappling with the aftermath of the Covid pandemic. Trump faces higher energy prices linked to the US-Israel conflict with Iran and the fallout from some of his own trade and immigration policies.
The political reality, however, is much the same. Voters are not judging the economy by GDP figures or record highs on the stock market. They are judging it by what they pay at the supermarket, petrol prices and whether they can keep up with the bills.
## Numbers only tell part of the story
US economic growth of 2.2% would be respectable by the standards of many wealthy economies. Germany, for example, has struggled to generate comparable momentum. And a stable US unemployment rate of 4.2% hardly suggests an economy in freefall.

But voters appear to be reading a different story - one in which Trump won the presidency promising to tackle the cost of living, only for prices to keep rising.
Part of the problem is that wage increases have not kept pace with prices. Average hourly earnings have risen 3% over the last year, while consumer prices have increased 3.7%.
That helps explain why polls continue to show voters giving Trump poor marks for his handling of the economy.
## Same language, different meanings
For Democrats, the dynamic feels familiar.
Celinda Lake, a veteran Democratic pollster, says the problem begins with the way economists and voters use the same language to mean different things.
"When economists say inflation comes down, they mean the rate of increase has come down," she says.
"When voters say inflation has come down, they mean the price has returned to what it was. Well, of course, it never returns to what it was."
