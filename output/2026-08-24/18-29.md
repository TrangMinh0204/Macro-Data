Title: Six million Somalis face acute hunger as drought and conflict worsen

Six million people in Somalia are facing acute hunger, with nearly two million children at emergency levels, as severe drought, conflict and falling humanitarian funding deepen what the UN describes as one of the world’s worst malnutrition crises. Medical charity MSF tells RFI it's sounding the alarm "before it's too late".
The World Food Programme (WFP), the UN agency providing food assistance, said around one third of Somalia’s population is now facing acute hunger, with [nearly two million children](https://x.com/WFPSomalia/status/2091480584203784449) expected to suffer from acute [malnutrition](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/malnutrition/) this year.
"Those are not abstract figures: they are families exhausting every option they have left to survive," the WFP said.
More than 200,000 people were displaced during the second quarter of 2026 as the country faced severe [drought](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/drought/). Poor rainfall over several seasons has led to water shortages, crop failures and livestock losses.
Higher fuel and food prices, made worse by the conflict in the [Middle East](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/middle-east/), have added to the pressure.
[Somalia](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/somalia/) is also facing an insurgency by the Islamist al-Shabaab group and localised armed clashes.
[Fears mount over resurgence of Al-Shabaab jihadists in Somalia](https://www.rfi.fr/en/international-news/20250327-fears-mount-over-resurgence-of-al-shabaab-jihadists-in-somalia)
The situation is particularly difficult in camps for displaced people in Baidoa, in the south-west of the country, where medical charity Doctors Without Borders (MSF) is working.
[MSF](https://www.msf.fr/) said one in two children in the camps was suffering from acute malnutrition, while one in four had severe acute malnutrition. One third of families are surviving on just one meal a day, MSF said.
The charity is also concerned about a lack of funding to respond to the crisis.
More than 80 percent of the number of patients admitted to its nutrition programme last year had already been admitted by this point in 2026, said MSF health adviser Mitchell Sangma.
"What is worrying is not only the numbers, but also the severity of these cases. These are very sick [children](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/children/), suffering from many medical complications," he told RFI.
"They arrive at a very advanced stage after having gone hungry for many months, and we are now seeing the consequences of the extreme food shortage."
Many are also suffering from epidemic diseases such as measles and diphtheria, he said.
[Under the same sky, climate change brings drought to Somalia and floods to Kenya](https://www.rfi.fr/en/africa/20260315-under-the-same-sky-climate-change-brings-drought-to-somalia-and-floods-to-kenya)
## Long queues for food and water
[Children](https://www.rfi.fr/en/tag/children/) treated in hospital are often readmitted after returning to camps where they have insufficient food and no access to safe drinking water, Sangma noted.
"Many are suffering from epidemic diseases, such as measles or diphtheria, which is very worrying. We receive these children, we treat them in our hospitals, we allow them to leave, but they end up being readmitted because they return to the same environment where they do not have access to enough food or safe drinking water," he said.
"It's very visible when you walk through the camps early in the morning. We see long lines of people with jerry cans waiting to receive water. Long lines of patients are waiting outside mobile clinics for their children to be treated. We are trying to sound the alarm about what we are seeing on the ground before it is too late."

Việc nghiên cứu một cách hệ thống và toàn diện về Trần Nhân Tông không chỉ có ý nghĩa tri ân tiền nhân, mà còn có ý nghĩa thời sự: tìm trong di sản tư tưởng của ngài những giá trị có khả năng “đối thoại” với các vấn đề của xã hội đương đại, từ xây dựng con người, đạo đức công vụ, đối ngoại, bản sắc dân tộc, đến sức khỏe tinh thần và giáo dục thế hệ trẻ.
Đặc biệt, khi Quần thể di tích và danh thắng Yên Tử-Vĩnh Nghiêm-Côn Sơn, Kiếp Bạc - không gian gắn liền với cuộc đời tu hành của Trần Nhân Tông và Phật giáo Trúc Lâm Yên Tử vừa được UNESCO ghi danh là Di sản văn hóa thế giới năm 2025, càng đặt ra yêu cầu cấp thiết phải tiếp tục nghiên cứu chuyên sâu, cập nhật và hệ thống hóa tri thức về Trần Nhân Tông và [Phật giáo Trúc Lâm](https://nhandan.vn/phat-hien-them-dau-tich-trong-khong-gian-phat-giao-truc-lam-yen-tu-tai-bac-ninh-post933057.html), làm cơ sở khoa học vững chắc cho công tác bảo tồn, phát huy giá trị di sản trong giai đoạn mới.
Hội thảo đã nhận được gần 60 tham luận của các tác giả, góp phần làm sáng tỏ tầm vóc và giá trị thời đại của di sản Trần Nhân Tông.
