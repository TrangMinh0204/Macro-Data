A powerful 6.8 magnitude earthquake hit southwest Japan on Tuesday afternoon, igniting fires near its epicenter, damaging buildings and highways, and injuring dozens, according to Japan’s public broadcaster.
Fire officials said they were racing to rescue people feared trapped inside a shopping mall in a southern neighborhood of Kumamoto City, after its second floor collapsed and injured at least two people. Aerial footage showed extensive damage to the building.
Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said the earthquake struck around 4:30 p.m. local time in Kumamoto Prefecture on Kyushu island, the southernmost of Japan’s main islands. According to local officials, the force of the quake collapsed buildings across the region, trapping people inside buildings, as fires broke out in Kumamoto City, the regional capital.
Japan’s Fire and Disaster Management Authority said local firefighters had responded after a [factory chimney collapsed](https://x.com/FDMA_JAPAN/status/2082121332096192820?s=20) in Yatsushiro City, in Kumamoto Province. Citing local officials, the public broadcaster, NHK, reported that two people were found “in a state of cardiopulmonary arrest,” and nine others were missing.
One person who was found in a collapsed house and taken to a hospital in Kumamoto has died, according to the Kumamoto Prefectural Police. At least 50 people were being treated for injuries at a hospital in Hikawa, a town to the south of the city, according to the public broadcaster NHK.
Aerial images taken by NHK showed that elevated sections of the Kyushu Expressway had broken into two pieces, as other footage showed a partially collapsed wall at Kumamoto Castle, a landmark in the city’s heart. Ms. Takaichi said the authorities were trying to assess the severity of damage to buildings and bridges.
The earthquake’s epicenter was near Uto, a coastal city around 30 miles from Kumamoto City in the same prefecture, according to the U.S. Geological Survey. The agency had initially reported it as a 7.1-magnitude quake before revising it down.
Local transportation links were affected by the force of the quake. Train services on Kyushu island were suspended, and Kumamoto International Airport, which serves the regional capital of more than 700,000 people, said it was closed. Across the region, the local police reported at least 12 collapsed houses, including four where people had been trapped.
The Fire and Disaster Management Authority said at least seven fires had broken out throughout the prefecture, mostly concentrated in Kumamoto City.
The Japan Meteorological Agency warned that earthquakes of a similar scale could occur in the next few days.
There were no reports of abnormalities at the Sendai Nuclear Power Plant, which is in Kagoshima Prefecture, near Kumamoto, Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said in a news briefing. The power plant has two nuclear reactors and supplies electricity to the Kyushu region.
